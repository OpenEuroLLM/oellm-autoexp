What can and cannot happen

Per head, each CKDA step applies M = (I − β k̂k̂ᵀ) · Diag(α) to the state. Here k̂ is the unit key, α ∈ [−1, 1]128 per channel (signed_sigmoid2, |α| ≥ e−5), and β ∈ [0, 2] per head (linear_beta_max 2). The characteristic polynomial factors as ∏(αᵢ − λ) · f(λ), with f(λ) = 1 − β Σ αᵢ k̂ᵢ² / (αᵢ − λ). Between two consecutive same-sign α's, f always crosses zero. This gives two hard constraints:

  • At most one complex-conjugate pair per head per step. The other 126 eigenvalues are real and sit near the α's; negative α's give reflections.
  • A pair needs β > 1 and α of both signs. For β ≤ 1, M is similar to the symmetric P½DP½. For α of one sign it is similar to a symmetric matrix too.

How it develops over training

One 1-node run per HF export of the main run, all with the same 96 documents. Early in training the model pushes β well below 1, though it starts near 1 (2σ(0) = 1). It then learns reflections, and with them sign-mixed decays and complex pairs, at a rate that hasn't flattened yet.

Where, at step

By layer

The first CKDA layers use complex pairs most, the last ones least. Use falls together with β > 1, the necessary condition. Only 1 % of α channels are negative, yet 16 % of the key mass lands on them: the writes aim at the few sign-flipping channels.

By head

The heads are strongly specialized. In most layers a few heads have a complex pair on more than half of the steps, while others have one almost never.

By token

Complex pairs cluster on clause-initial words and document boundaries, where the state gets rewritten. They are rarest on predictable continuations in code and markup. The figure is the fraction over all 42 layers × 16 heads, for tokens seen at least 20 times.

What the pair looks like

Density of the upper eigenvalue of the pair in the complex plane (real part −1…1, imaginary part 0…1, log scale). In every layer the mass sits inside |λ| < 0.5, spread over angles near 90°–135°. That is the strongly damped key direction (the delta rule's erase), coupled across the sign boundary. A few layers have thin tails toward |λ| ≈ 0.8–0.9.

Does the model need the complex part?

Ablations on the same documents. The first three act on all steps. The rest act only on the complex steps, where both changes remove the pair, or on a matched random set of real sign-mixed steps as a control.

β → 1 at the complex steps costs about eight times more than α → |α| at the same steps. Both remove the pair, so the model needs those steps' large β (an overshooting erase along k̂), not their rotation. Caveat: β also scales that step's write (β k̂ vᵀ), so this ablation does not isolate the transition.

Method and limits

  • Hook. FLA's chunk_kda inside fla.layers.complex_kda_layer is wrapped, so the analysis sees exactly the k, g, β and sign the kernel gets. The model runs from the HF exports with FP8 experts and the Engram tables in host memory, on one 4×H100 node.
  • Data. 16 documents of ≥ 256 tokens from each of six v8ml-valid holdouts, BOS plus up to 8191 tokens (the phase-1 context): tokens.
  • Per-step spectrum only. Products over several steps can rotate even with α ≥ 0, because two reflections compose to a rotation. That multi-step effect is not measured here.
  • Exactness. Solver vs torch.linalg.eigvals: 512 random transitions and about 1500 real steps per run, no existence disagreement, max error ~1e-12.
  • Runs. p264 pool runs (host003, and host002 for 18000) and one acc_debug job for 21000. Code: experiment/015_ckda_complex_eigenvalues, config ckda_spectra_mn5.
